    1 · The research landscape

    Signal control research has moved in waves. Each wave kept the previous one as a baseline, and the newest wave, LLM agents, increasingly uses the older ones as tools.

    WaveIdeaRepresentative work
    Adaptive systems (1980s-)Measure demand, re-optimise cycle, split, offsetSCOOT, SCATS (see the companion labs)
    Max-pressure (2013-)Serve the largest in-minus-out queue; provable maximum throughput, review , hardware-in-the-loop
    Deep RL / MARL (2019-)Learn the policy from reward; agents coordinate through graphs or pressurePressLight , CoLight , MPLight , FRAP , AttendLight
    LLM as tool user (2023-)A language model orchestrates simulators and controllers for an operatorTrafficGPT , Open-TI
    LLM as controller (2023-)The LLM reads the traffic state and picks the phase, with a reasonLLMLight , LA-Light , CoLLMLight
    Hybrids (2024-)Fast RL/rules for routine cases, LLM reasoning or critique for hard onesiLLM-TSC , VLMLight , HeraldLight , PromptGAT
    Signals + CAVsCV data as sensor; eco-driving, GLOSA; mixed autonomy, , ,
    Every LLM controller in the reviewed literature was evaluated in simulation only; none reports a field deployment. Headline gains of about 17.5-20.4 % in waiting or travel time are reported against RL baselines in the authors' own setups.

    2 · How to evaluate a new idea fairly

    1. Strong baselines. Always compare with well-tuned actuated control and max-pressure, not only fixed time.
    2. Same everything else. Same network, demand, seeds, yellow/all-red, minimum green. In our code every controller drives the same Junction class.
    3. Several seeds. Report mean and spread over at least 3 random demand patterns.
    4. More than delay. Stops, CO2, the 95th-percentile and longest wait (fairness), safety violations.
    5. Stress tests. Sensor faults, message latency and loss, demand different from training, bad model outputs.
    6. Ablation. Turn one component off at a time to show what actually helps.
    7. Honesty. Publish negative results; several of ours are.

    6 · Open research questions

    1. Verified LLM supervision: can an LLM supervising a max-pressure or RL controller be guaranteed never to break minimum green or a maximum pedestrian wait, and what does that cost?
    2. Faithful explanations: do an LLM's stated reasons match what actually drove the decision? How would we measure faithfulness with engineers?
    3. Which sim-to-real gap dominates for LLM vs RL controllers: observation, action, transition or reward?
    4. Robustness under combined faults: detector outages, V2X latency and noisy prompts in one stress-test protocol.
    5. Scalable coordination: when should a cheap local policy escalate to expensive reasoning under a fixed compute budget?
    6. Equity-aware control: can operators state people-centric goals in plain language and compile them safely into constraints?
    7. Low-penetration CAV co-control: below about 25 % CAVs, can joint signal and eco-driving control beat actuated control?
    8. Path to certification: what evidence would a road authority need to approve a learning or LLM controller?

      8 · Recap

      1. Waves: adaptive → max-pressure → deep RL → LLM tool users → LLM controllers → hybrids; all mostly in simulation.
      2. Fair evaluation: strong baselines, same rules, seeds, many metrics, stress tests, ablations, honest negatives.
      3. Gaps: sim-to-real, safety, hallucination, explainability, robustness, scale and latency, equity, mixed autonomy, predictability, benchmarks, human factors.
      4. Our experiments: on a short corridor messages help a little and latency barely matters (test more seeds); shields stop bad LLM answers but cost some delay; CAV-only sensing works but degrades at low penetration; a little fairness weight is free; committing to signal changes gives fewer stops but, in our implementation, costs more delay and CO2 than it saves.
      5. Future: layered agents with a fast base, slow reasoning, critic, formal shield, digital twin, V2X and people in the loop.
